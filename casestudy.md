## Background

UPI (Unified Payments Interface) fraud is one of the fastest-growing cybercrimes in India, with the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal recording tens of thousands of related complaints every month. College students — many using UPI independently for the first time — are among the most frequently targeted groups.

The following is a composite realistic scenario based on documented patterns reported by cybercrime.gov.in and news investigations.

---

## Case Study: "The OLX Seller Scam"

**Victim:** Arjun, a second-year engineering student in Pune, selling his old laptop on OLX for ₹18,000.

### Step-by-Step: How It Happened

**Step 1 – Contact**
A buyer named "Captain Rahul Singh" (fraudster using a fake military identity for trust) contacted Arjun through OLX. He expressed immediate interest, said he was posted at a cantonment, and couldn't meet in person — but would send a courier and pay via UPI.

**Step 2 – Building Trust**
The fraudster shared a fake ID card and was extremely polite and professional. He sent ₹1 (a test amount) to Arjun's UPI ID, proving he "had the payment ready." Arjun felt secure.

**Step 3 – The Trap**
The fraudster said he would send ₹18,000 but needed Arjun to "accept" the payment via a QR code he shared on WhatsApp. He said it was a "bank transfer request" for large amounts.

**Step 4 – The Loss**
Arjun scanned the QR code — not understanding that QR codes on UPI are used to *send* money, not receive it. By scanning it, he authorised a payment of ₹9,000 from his own account. The fraudster sent two more "confirmation" QR codes, each draining another ₹5,000 before Arjun realised what had happened. Total loss: ₹19,000.

**Step 5 – Disappearance**
The fraudster blocked Arjun immediately. The phone number was a temporary SIM. The account the money went to was a mule account — already frozen and untraceable.

---

## Who Is Targeted?

First-time UPI users, students selling items online, people in financial pressure, and anyone unfamiliar with how QR-based payments work. Fraudsters specifically seek out listings from students because they tend to respond faster and are less likely to verify.

---

## Consequences

Financial loss (often in the ₹5,000–₹50,000 range for students), emotional distress, and almost zero recovery in most cases unless reported within the first few hours. Bank reversal is rare once money has moved through mule accounts.
