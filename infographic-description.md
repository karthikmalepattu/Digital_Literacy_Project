## What My Infographic Shows

The infographic is titled **"Be Digitally Smart"** and is divided into five visual sections, each using a distinct icon and colour block:

1. **What is Digital Literacy?**
   Defined as the ability to find, evaluate, create, and communicate information using digital technologies — not just knowing how to use a phone or laptop, but knowing *why* and *when* to use each tool.

2. **Useful Digital Tools for Students**
   Highlights tools across three categories: learning (Google Scholar, YouTube, NPTEL), productivity (Notion, Google Docs, Grammarly), and professional growth (GitHub, LinkedIn, Kaggle).

3. **Safe Internet Practices**
   Covers three non-negotiable habits: using strong passwords + a password manager, enabling two-factor authentication (2FA), and never clicking unknown links — especially in emails or WhatsApp messages.

4. **Professional Online Presence**
   A side-by-side visual showing what a poor LinkedIn profile looks like versus a strong one — emphasising a clear photo, headline, and relevant skills even in first year.

5. **Email Etiquette at a Glance**
   A mini checklist format showing the five parts of a proper professional email: Subject → Greeting → Body → Sign-off → Signature.

## Reflection 

I used Canva to create this infographic because it offered the most control over layout without requiring any design experience. I started with a blank template and added each section manually, which ended up teaching me more about visual hierarchy than any pre-built template would have.

The most interesting part was designing the "Professional Online Presence" section. I had never consciously thought about what my digital footprint looks like to someone who doesn't know me — a faculty member, an internship recruiter, or even a peer from another college. Putting it into a side-by-side format made the difference very visible.

The most difficult part was figuring out how to present the "Safe Internet Practices" section without making it feel like a warning poster. I kept the tone positive — framing it as habits smart students build, not a list of scary threats.

Overall, this task shifted how I think about communication: that a well-designed one-page visual can carry more impact than three paragraphs of text, and that design is itself a form of digital literacy.
