**Reflection 

For Part A, I chose HackerRank over CodeChef because its interface is cleaner for beginners and it has structured learning paths by language and skill level. The Python domain in particular has a logical progression from print statements to lambda functions — which aligns well with what I want to study in my Cyber Security programme (Python is the dominant scripting language in the field).

For Part B, building the Google Form turned out to be more thoughtful than I expected. Designing a good quiz question is genuinely harder than answering one. I had to think about what makes a wrong answer plausible enough to be a useful distractor, rather than just obviously wrong. The short-answer question at the end was a deliberate choice — I wanted at least one question that forced reflection rather than recall.

Connecting the Form to Google Sheets was a new thing for me. The idea that every response automatically populates a structured spreadsheet — in real time, with timestamps — made me understand immediately why Google Workspace is used in every office environment. It is genuinely powerful for collaborative data collection, and I can already see myself using Forms for peer feedback or study group sign-ups throughout my degree.

These tools will be part of how I work for the next four years, and probably beyond.
