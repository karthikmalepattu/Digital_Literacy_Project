> This checklist is designed specifically for first-year students in India who are using UPI,
> social media, and online platforms independently for the first time.

---

## ✅ General Online Safety

- [ ] **1. Use a unique, strong password for every account.**
  Don't reuse passwords across your Gmail, college portal, GitHub, and social media. Use a password manager like Bitwarden (free) to manage them securely.

- [ ] **2. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on every important account.**
  Gmail, GitHub, LinkedIn, and your UPI app should all have 2FA turned on. Use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator or Authy) rather than SMS where possible.

- [ ] **3. Never click links in SMS or WhatsApp messages that claim to be from banks, UPI apps, or government portals.**
  Phishing links are designed to look exactly like real sites. Always type the URL directly into your browser or use the official app.

- [ ] **4. Verify unknown callers before sharing any information.**
  No legitimate bank, NPCI, or government agency will call you and ask for your UPI PIN, OTP, or Aadhaar number over the phone. Hang up and call the official helpline to verify.

- [ ] **5. Regularly check your app permissions.**
  Once a month, review which apps on your phone have access to your camera, microphone, contacts, and location. Revoke any permissions that seem unnecessary.

- [ ] **6. Use only official, trusted Wi-Fi networks.**
  Avoid doing any financial transactions on public or unknown Wi-Fi (hostel common area routers, café networks). Use your mobile data for banking.

---

## 💸 UPI & Financial Safety (Mandatory Tips)

- [ ] **7. Remember: scanning a QR code on UPI sends money — it never receives it.**
  If anyone asks you to "scan a QR code to receive a refund or payment," that is a scam. There is no such thing as a receiving QR code in UPI. Only the payer scans.

- [ ] **8. Never share your UPI PIN, OTP, or mPIN with anyone — not even someone claiming to be from your bank or PhonePe/GPay support.**
  These credentials are yours alone. No bank employee or app support agent will ever ask for them. Anyone who does is a fraudster.

## 📣 If Something Goes Wrong – Report Immediately

| Channel | How to Reach | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **National Cyber Crime Portal** | [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in) | Online fraud, cyberbullying, hacking, identity theft |
| **Cyber Crime Helpline** | **1930** (24×7, toll-free) | Financial fraud — call within hours for best chance of recovery |
| **Your Bank's Helpline** | On back of debit card / bank app | Freeze account, dispute transaction |
| **VIT Campus Security / Warden** | Through your hostel or college app | Report cyberbullying or harassment involving fellow students |

---

> ⏱️ **Most important thing if you are defrauded:** Report to 1930 **within the first few hours**.
> The sooner you report, the higher the chance the transaction can be flagged or reversed
> before money is moved out of reach.
