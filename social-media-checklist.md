## ✅ Do's

| # | Do This | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Keep your profiles professional and searchable.** Use your real name, a clear photo, and a sensible username across platforms. | Recruiters, faculty, and collaborators search for you by name. First impressions happen before you're in the room. |
| 2 | **Think before you post — the 24-hour rule.** If you're reacting to something emotionally, wait a day before posting. | Posts made in frustration or anger often cause damage that is hard to undo, even after deletion. |
| 3 | **Give credit when you share someone else's work.** If you share a post, image, or idea that isn't yours, tag or mention the original creator. | It's basic respect, and it also signals integrity to anyone watching your online behaviour. |
| 4 | **Review your privacy settings on every platform at least once a semester.** Know what's public and what's not. | Apps quietly update their privacy defaults. What you assumed was private may no longer be. |
| 5 | **Use social media to learn and connect — not just consume.** Follow researchers, professionals, and institutions in your field of interest. | LinkedIn and Twitter are full of people sharing real insights in Cyber Security, AI, and tech. This is free, high-quality education. |

---

## ❌ Don'ts

| # | Don't Do This | Why It's a Problem |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Don't post anything you wouldn't say to a faculty member's face.** Complaints about college, professors, or peers can resurface at the worst moments. | Screenshots travel far. What seems like a private group chat or a funny story can end up being read by the very person it's about. |
| 2 | **Don't share your location in real time.** Avoid posting "I'm at X place right now" or tagging your current location in stories. | Real-time location sharing is a safety risk and also signals when your home or hostel room is empty. |
| 3 | **Don't engage in or ignore cyberbullying.** Neither participating in online pile-ons nor staying silent when you see someone being targeted is acceptable. | Both participation and silence make the behaviour worse. Report, block, and reach out to the person being targeted. |
| 4 | **Don't use the same password for your social media accounts as for your college email or banking apps.** | A single breach on a low-security platform can cascade into every account you own if you reuse passwords. |
| 5 | **Don't overshare personal details like your phone number, home address, or daily routine.** | This information, combined across multiple posts, can be enough for a bad actor to profile and target you — a technique called OSINT (Open Source Intelligence). |

> Build it like one.
